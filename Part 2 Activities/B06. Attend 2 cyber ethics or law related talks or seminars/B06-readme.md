# B6. Attend 2 cyber ethics/law related talks/seminars.

## 1st Seminar – NAB Customer Cybersecurity and Fraud Webinar

For this activity, the first seminar I attended was NAB’s Customer Cybersecurity and Fraud Webinar on 12 May 2026. This webinar was part of NAB’s personal cybersecurity awareness sessions, which are designed to help customers better understand online fraud, scams, and how to stay safe in everyday digital life. The session focused on the current security threat environment, how criminals target individuals and families, common scams people should be aware of, and practical steps to stay protected. I found this seminar useful because it approached cybersecurity in a very practical way. Instead of focusing only on technical systems, it concentrated on the kinds of threats ordinary people are most likely to face, such as phishing, impersonation, scam messages, and social engineering. That made the session feel very relevant, because these are the kinds of attacks that can affect almost anyone who uses online banking, email, or mobile devices.

What I liked most about this webinar was that it showed how much of cybersecurity is connected to human behaviour. A lot of scams do not depend on breaking into systems in a highly technical way, but instead on making people panic, trust the wrong message, or act too quickly. The webinar seemed to be built around exactly that idea: helping customers recognise warning signs, slow down, and make safer decisions. I found that especially meaningful because it reminded me that cybersecurity is not only about software and tools, but also about awareness, judgment, and communication. Fraud can cause real harm to people’s finances, confidence, and sense of safety, so I felt this seminar highlighted an important ethical side of cybersecurity as well. Overall, attending this session gave me a better appreciation of how banks try to educate the public and reduce risks before damage happens, which I think is a very important part of cyber safety.

## 2nd Seminar – IChemE Webinar: How Cybersecurity Threats Influence Control Systems and Process Safety

The second seminar I attended was IChemE’s “How Cybersecurity Threats Influence Control Systems and Process Safety” webinar on 14 May 2026. This session was presented by Paresh Kerai from MWS Risk and focused on how cyber threats can affect industrial control systems, operational technology, and process safety in high-risk environments. I found this seminar especially interesting because it approached cybersecurity from a much broader and more serious perspective than the usual discussions around scams or account protection. The talk showed that cyber incidents in industrial environments are not just about data breaches or system downtime. In some cases, they can directly affect equipment, physical operations, and safety outcomes. That made the session feel very different from a general cyber awareness seminar and much more connected to real-world consequences.

One of the most memorable parts of the webinar was the idea that process failures and cyberattacks can lead to the same dangerous result. The example that stood out to me was the scenario involving loss of cooling to a reactor. In a traditional process safety context, that kind of failure might come from a mechanical issue or an operator mistake. In a cyber context, however, the same outcome could happen if malware targeted the PLCs controlling the cooling pumps and even manipulated the HMI so operators believed everything was still normal. I found this point extremely powerful because it made the consequences of cyberattacks feel much more real and serious. It showed that in industrial settings, a cyberattack can go far beyond stolen information and actually become a safety issue that threatens equipment, the environment, and human life.

Another part of the webinar that I found very useful was how it connected cybersecurity with existing process safety methods and standards. The seminar explained that cyber risks should not be treated as a completely separate problem, but instead need to be integrated into the broader way organisations already think about safety, risk assessment, design, validation, and protection layers. I found that very important because it showed that cybersecurity in these environments is not just an extra technical feature added at the end. It has to be built into the system properly and considered alongside other forms of risk. Overall, this seminar left a strong impression on me because it showed how deeply cybersecurity can influence industrial systems and why it needs to be taken seriously not only as an IT issue, but also as a matter of safety, responsibility, and professional practice.
