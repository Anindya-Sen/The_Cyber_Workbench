# B12. Discover two bias cases when using a generative AI system.


For this activity, I wanted to explore whether a generative AI system could still show bias by producing outputs that follow common stereotypes. I used ChatGPT for this and designed prompts that were connected to familiar social assumptions. I chose this activity because I was curious whether AI systems today are fully free from stereotypes or whether they can still reflect older biased ideas in the way they respond. Instead of only reading about bias in theory, I wanted to test it directly through actual prompts and observe the outputs for myself. This made the activity much more practical, because I was not only discussing AI bias as a concept, but actually trying to discover it while using a generative AI system.


## 1. Bias case in image generation – “good driver at a traffic stop”

The first bias case I discovered was related to the stereotype that women are not good drivers. To test this, I asked ChatGPT to generate an image of a good driver at a traffic stop. I chose this prompt very deliberately because I wanted to see whether the model would respond in a neutral way or whether it would still follow the stereotype that a “good driver” is more likely to be male. When the image was generated, the driver shown was male, even though the prompt itself did not ask for a man. That is what made this feel like a clear bias case to me. The output did not simply reflect the words I used — it seemed to fill in the missing details by following a social stereotype. This showed me that even image-generation outputs can still reflect biased assumptions, especially when the prompt includes a role or characteristic that society has often linked unfairly to one gender.



## 2. Bias case in story generation – men linked to achievement and leadership

The second bias case I discovered was related to the stereotype that men are the highest achievers and are more naturally associated with higher managerial positions. To test this, I asked ChatGPT to generate a story in a way that would show whether it followed that same stereotype or not. As I expected, the response leaned into that pattern and reflected the idea that men were more closely linked to high achievement and leadership. I found this to be another clear bias case because the story seemed to follow a familiar stereotype rather than presenting leadership and success in a more balanced way. What I found especially important here is that the bias appeared in a written response, not in an image. This made me realise that bias in generative AI is not limited to one output type. It can show up both visually and in written content, depending on how the model fills in assumptions behind the prompt.

Overall, I found this activity very useful because it helped me understand that generative AI systems can still reproduce biased patterns and stereotypes instead of always responding in a completely neutral way. The two cases I discovered were different in format, one through image generation and one through story generation, but both pointed toward the same larger issue: AI systems can still reflect social assumptions that already exist in the world and in the data they are trained on. Through this activity I was able to discover bias cases while directly using a generative AI system, and it also made me more aware that AI outputs should not automatically be treated as neutral or fair without question.
