# B11. Talk to 2 cybersecurity experts from the industry and find out their latest projects.

## 1. Conversation with Liam O’Shannessy – Executive Director at CyberCX

For this activity, one of the cybersecurity professionals I spoke with was Liam O’Shannessy, who is an Executive Director at CyberCX. I was fortunate enough to meet him during the Students of Cyber May Event with CyberCX, and after the event I had the opportunity to speak with him personally. I found this especially valuable because it gave me the chance to hear directly from someone working at a senior level in the cybersecurity industry. During our conversation, one of the most interesting things I learned about was his latest work and observations around AI deepfakes. He explained how quickly this area is developing and how surprisingly little information is now needed to generate a convincing deepfake. That part stood out to me because it made the threat feel much more immediate and realistic than it often sounds in general discussion.
What I found particularly interesting was that he also mentioned some of the current practical limitations of deepfakes, such as the fact that they still often struggle with beards and with people who do not have much hair. That detail made the whole conversation feel much more genuine and grounded in real industry observation, because it showed that this is not just a vague conversation about AI being dangerous, but something professionals are actively studying, testing, and trying to understand in detail. Speaking with him helped me realise that deepfakes are becoming a serious cybersecurity problem because they can be used for impersonation, fraud, deception, and social engineering, and the fact that less input data is needed now makes the risk even greater. Overall, I found this conversation extremely valuable because it exposed me to a very current and rapidly growing area of cybersecurity directly through someone who is actively working in the field.

## 2. Conversation with Jevon Ang – SOC Analyst at Huntress

The second cybersecurity professional I spoke with was Jevon Ang, who is a very experienced SOC analyst at Huntress. I had the chance to speak with him personally and learned about one of the latest projects connected to his work. The latest project context I learned from him was tied to a threat investigation involving Komari, a monitoring tool that includes a built-in interactive reverse shell. From the incident description, attackers had used SSLVPN credentials, then used smbexec to help get RDP open before dropping Komari. What made this especially interesting is that it showed how attackers are now increasingly abusing tools that look legitimate on the surface instead of always relying on obviously malicious software.
I found this conversation very useful because it gave me a much clearer picture of how modern cyber threats actually look in practice. Instead of only thinking about malware as something clearly suspicious, this example showed that attackers can weaponise normal-looking monitoring tools and hide inside activity that may seem less alarming at first. That makes detection much harder and shows how important it is for SOC analysts to understand context and behaviour, not just obvious warning signs. On top of that, my personal conversation with Jevon was also really meaningful because he shared advice with me about the field itself, including pathways into cybersecurity and how to think about progressing in it over time. Overall, I found this activity very worthwhile because both experts were working on very current and relevant problems, and speaking with them helped me understand not only their latest projects, but also the wider direction and reality of the cybersecurity industry.
