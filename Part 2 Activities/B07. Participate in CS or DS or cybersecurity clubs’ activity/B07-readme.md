# B7. Participate in CS/DS/cybersecurity clubs’ activity.


For this activity, I participated in a cybersecurity-related club event organised by the University Computer Club (UCC). I attended the tech talk held on 24th April 2026, presented by Mark Tearle, titled “Into the Cuckoo’s Egg.” I was there in person during the session, and it was a genuinely memorable experience. It felt like a very engaging mix of cybersecurity history, investigation, system-level thinking, and storytelling. The topic itself already stood out to me because it was based around the early internet era, a very small accounting discrepancy, and how that tiny issue eventually led into something much bigger and more serious.

What I especially liked about Mark Tearle’s talk was the way he explained things so clearly. He spent time talking about systems, how they were structured, and how records inside those systems could reveal much more than they first seemed to. One part that caught my attention was when he discussed records of UNIX systems accounting and localised accounting scripts, which made the whole story feel much more technical and real rather than just historical. He also mentioned some prominent and respected people in the field, which made the talk feel richer and showed how this area of computing and cybersecurity has been shaped by people with deep technical understanding and experience. That part made me realise that cybersecurity is not only about modern tools and threats, but also about understanding how systems behave, how records are kept, and how very small irregularities can reveal major security issues.

Another reason this event felt valuable to me is that Mark himself is a senior software engineer at QCIF, so hearing him speak gave the session even more weight. Sitting there and listening to him explain these ideas in front of the audience made the whole night feel much more genuine and worthwhile. I could follow how the talk connected computing knowledge, system behaviour, and cybersecurity investigation in a way that was both interesting and educational. Overall, I felt that attending this club activity was a very good experience because it exposed me to ideas beyond normal classroom learning.
