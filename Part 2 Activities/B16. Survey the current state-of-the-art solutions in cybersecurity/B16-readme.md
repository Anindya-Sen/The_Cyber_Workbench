# B16. Survey the current state-of-the-art solutions in cybersecurity.


For this activity, I surveyed the current state-of-the-art solutions in cybersecurity and tried to understand where the field is heading right now. What stood out to me most is that modern cybersecurity is no longer only about putting a firewall around a system and hoping that is enough. The strongest current solutions are much more connected and proactive. They focus on areas such as identity protection, zero trust, unified detection and response, AI-assisted defence, and reducing internet exposure. In simple terms, the best cybersecurity solutions today are designed to verify trust more carefully, detect suspicious behaviour earlier, and reduce opportunities for attackers before damage happens. This made me realise that modern cybersecurity is becoming much more about continuous protection rather than one-time setup.

One of the clearest state-of-the-art directions I found was phishing-resistant authentication, especially through passkeys and similar modern login methods. I found this especially important because passwords are still one of the weakest parts of everyday security. Passkeys are now being promoted by major organisations such as CISA, Google, and Microsoft as a much stronger alternative because they reduce phishing risk and remove the need to type reusable passwords. What makes this approach stand out is that it improves both security and usability at the same time. Instead of remembering another password, users can sign in using a fingerprint, face scan, or device PIN. That makes authentication not only easier, but also harder for attackers to steal or trick out of users.

Another major direction I noticed is the rise of unified detection and response platforms, especially XDR. This is important because modern attacks usually do not stay in just one place. They can move across email, identities, endpoints, cloud services, and apps very quickly. Because of that, strong modern solutions now try to combine visibility across all of these areas instead of treating them separately. I found this idea very useful because it shows how cybersecurity is shifting away from isolated tools and toward systems that can connect signals together and give defenders a clearer picture of what is really happening.

I also found that AI-assisted security operations are now a major part of current cybersecurity. Tools such as Microsoft Security Copilot show that defenders are increasingly using AI to help process huge amounts of security information, understand alerts more quickly, and support investigation and response. This stood out to me because AI is now influencing cybersecurity from both sides. Attackers are using it to improve scams, deception, and automation, while defenders are using it to work faster and make sense of more complex threats. This makes AI one of the most important parts of the current cyber landscape.

Another strong trend I came across is browser and exposure-focused security. Today, so much work happens through browsers, cloud services, and internet-facing systems that these areas have become major security boundaries on their own. Guidance from organisations like CISA shows that reducing internet exposure is a very important part of modern defence, while newer browser-security approaches try to stop phishing, malware, and web-based threats before they can reach the user or the endpoint. I found this especially interesting because it shows how cybersecurity is adapting to the way people now actually work and use technology.

Overall, this survey helped me understand that the state of the art in cybersecurity is moving toward stronger identity controls, smarter detection, more automation, and more proactive defence. What makes these solutions modern is not just that they are new, but that they directly respond to the kinds of threats that are most common today, such as phishing, identity abuse, internet exposure, and complex multi-stage attacks. I found this activity very useful because it gave me a much clearer picture of how cybersecurity is evolving and what kinds of solutions are becoming most important in real-world practice.
